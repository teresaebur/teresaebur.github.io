Medspa Instagram reach drops 81% after paused ad spend on Meta platforms

Before and after metrics were collected and analyzed across 15.5 weeks on Instagram and Facebook

Teresa Burrell, ButterEight

The question

The implementation of a dedicated social media and brand manager alongside a third-party medspa software and compliance system, required co-founders to ask: how much of social media reach is due to ad spend?

My role

My role, via ButterEight, was managing branding, organic social media outreach (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and Google), and towards the end of the contract, influencer relations and partnerships. My contract started 6 months after ads were independently run via the medspa’s third-party system. From this perspective, I consulted on potential dips in brand reach, views, and profile visits if ads were paused. The founders ultimately decided to move forward with pausing all medspa ads across Meta platforms and paid ad support ended early February, the data reflects that after February 6th.

What I did

This case study was formed by pulling Meta analytics between August 2025 to April 2026. The data between January 1st and February 5th are before the ads were paused and February 6th to April 20th is data after the ads were paused. Ads were created, configured, and monitored by the aforementioned third-party platform on Instagram and Facebook. All data points from the analytics export were transferred to an Excel sheet that included the daily average of reach, Facebook views, Facebook unique viewers, Instagram views, link clicks (Facebook + Instagram), Instagram interactions, and Instagram profile visits before and after ad pause.

What I found

By separating the pre and post ad pause numbers, I found an 81% drop in daily reach on Instagram, 84% drop in Facebook views, and a 91% drop in unique viewers on Facebook.

What changed

In lieu of third-party ad spend, I decided to increase micro-influencer partnerships and increase the visibility of the co-founders in the marketing strategy. Both of these strategies fit into the new marketing budget. Influencer partnerships compensated a handful of local micro lifestyle and wellness influencers who received an IV hydration session in exchange for one short-form video and 2 story posts: 1 before and 1 after their hydration session. The co-founders were made the face of the brand by including them in short-form videos showcasing their expertise and a science-backed approach to treatments.

Results

This strategy was consistent throughout March. The data showcases a rise in Instagram profile visits and new followers compared with the pre-pause daily average, by 58% and 27% respectively. However, Instagram profile visits and new followers dropped below baseline in April. Instagram reach and Facebook views never recovered to where they were before ads were paused in February.

Limitations and next steps

A big limitation in this study was the time frame of data collected before and after the ad pause. By the start of my contract with the medspa, they had already had the third-party company running their ads for 6 months. When I came into the role, ads would then run for a total of a year before they were paused from February to mid-May. My contract ended in May. The data I have after the pause was only 2.5 months. One thing I recommended when I left was to bring in a brand manager alongside someone who could manage a dedicated paid media strategy in a way that aligned with the medspa budget. This case reinforced the importance of stating that a well rounded social media marketing strategy includes paid advertisements and an organic social media push in conjunction.

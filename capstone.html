How is social media influencing changing branding from a transactional process into an entrepreneurial practice?

Undergraduate capstone, George Mason University, May 2026

Teresa Burrell

Summary

My undergraduate capstone analyzed Alex Cooper’s career against two academic frameworks: the Influencer-Entrepreneurship Model and Fink’s Influencer Branding Performance Triangle. As the creator economy reaches into most forms of marketing, it’s worth asking what actually builds a creator’s audience. Using engagement metrics from Spotify, the YouTube API, and Social Blade, I used R to chart how engagement changed as Cooper moved through each stage of the influencer-entrepreneurship model.

Across her career from 2021 to 2026, the data is consistent with the factors in Fink’s triangle, including authenticity, strong brand collaborations, and transparency. Consistency also mattered: engagement spiked around career milestones, especially as Cooper built her media network. In the three years of data I collected (2023 to 2026), her personal Instagram grew from 2.4 million to 6.6 million followers (up 175%), while the Call Her Daddy account grew from 1.9 million to 3.7 million (up 95%). Her personal following grew faster than the brand’s, which suggests her personal reach drove much of the brand’s growth. Over the same period, the Call Her Daddy YouTube channel moved from the competence stage to the reward stage, with about 38 times as many views.

I collected views, posting frequency, posting volume, and followers. The public data could not show who the audience is, how much they listen, or how much revenue the brand earned.
